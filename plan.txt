You are a senior iOS engineer, product designer, endurance-sports product manager, data architect, QA engineer, and technical writer. Build a production-quality, native iPhone application for managing a complete 36-week full-distance triathlon training plan.

The application should feel like an official Apple application: quiet, polished, highly legible, native, responsive, accessible, and intentionally designed. It must not look like a generic fitness dashboard, a React web page inside an app, a gaming interface, or an AI-generated collection of colorful cards.

The working product name is **Endurance**. Keep the name isolated in configuration so it can be renamed later.

## 1. Primary objective

Create a personal iPhone application that:

* Stores a complete 36-week full-distance triathlon training plan.
* Maps training-plan days to actual calendar dates based on a configurable start date or race date.
* Shows exactly what the athlete must do today.
* Sends useful local reminders for workouts, fueling, recovery, preparation, and weekly review.
* Lets the athlete complete, skip, reschedule, shorten, replace, or annotate workouts.
* Tracks progress without encouraging unsafe catch-up behavior.
* Works offline.
* Preserves user data between launches.
* Can be installed on a physical iPhone.
* Can later be distributed through TestFlight.
* Looks and behaves like a first-party Apple app.

The app is initially intended for one 20-year-old athlete training for a first full-distance triathlon over approximately nine months. The default objective is to finish safely and consistently, not to reach an elite finishing time.

Do not present the app as medical care or as a replacement for a qualified coach or clinician.

## 4. Product principles

The product must follow these principles:

### Native first

Use standard SwiftUI components, navigation behavior, sheets, menus, pickers, toggles, lists, forms, alerts, confirmation dialogs, swipe actions, context menus, toolbars, and system transitions.

### Calm, not gamified

Avoid:

* Confetti.
* Constant streak pressure.
* Neon gradients.
* Excessive rings.
* Giant motivational slogans.
* Punishment language.
* Red warning states for ordinary missed workouts.
* Arbitrary experience points.
* Fake social competition.

Progress should feel informative rather than judgmental.

### Today is the center

The most important question is:

“What should I do today?”

The Today screen must answer that within one second.

### Safety over completion statistics

Never tell the athlete to stack missed hard workouts or complete extra volume merely to preserve a completion rate.

### Progressive disclosure

Show essential information first. Let users expand for intervals, technique cues, fueling instructions, and notes.

### Offline reliability

The plan, completion history, settings, and notifications must continue to work without an account or internet connection.

### Privacy

Keep personal fitness information on-device by default. Do not add analytics, advertising, tracking, or external accounts to the first version.

## 14. Adaptation and missed-workout rules

The first version does not need an AI coach. It does need safe deterministic rules.

When a workout is missed:

* Do not automatically move it.
* Do not automatically stack it with another hard workout.
* Ask the athlete what to do.
* Offer skip, move, shorten, or replace with recovery.
* Warn when a move creates consecutive high-stress sessions.
* Prefer preserving long-term consistency over preserving one workout.

Define workout stress categories:

* Recovery.
* Easy.
* Moderate.
* Hard.
* Long.
* Race-specific.

Define conflict rules, such as:

* Avoid consecutive hard runs.
* Avoid moving a long run directly after another demanding run.
* Avoid placing maximal bike intervals immediately before the longest ride.
* Avoid restoring missed volume during a recovery week without explicit action.
* Never prescribe training through reported pain or illness.

When the user reports illness or injury symptoms, provide a conservative prompt encouraging rest and appropriate professional advice. Do not diagnose.

## 16. Training content

Create a complete 36-week training-plan seed file.

The plan must include every day and every session, but avoid pretending there is one universally correct plan.

Organize it into progressive phases with planned recovery weeks.

A reasonable initial structure to research and validate:

* Weeks 1–4: Foundation and routine.
* Weeks 5–8: Base 1.
* Weeks 9–12: Base 2.
* Weeks 13–16: Build 1.
* Weeks 17–20: Build 2.
* Weeks 21–24: Endurance specificity.
* Weeks 25–28: Race-specific build.
* Weeks 29–32: Peak-specific work.
* Week 33: Final major simulation or peak.
* Weeks 34–35: Taper.
* Week 36: Race week.

Do not blindly follow these ranges if current research supports a better structure.

Plan requirements:

* Swimming, cycling, running, strength, mobility, recovery, brick sessions, and race preparation.
* Gradual progression.
* Recurring recovery weeks.
* Swim technique emphasis early.
* Open-water skills when appropriate.
* Bike handling and equipment practice.
* Progressive bricks.
* Long-session fueling practice.
* Transition practice.
* Race-equipment rehearsals.
* Reduced strength load during the most specific phase.
* Taper that lowers volume while preserving some intensity.
* No full marathon training run.
* No implication that the athlete must complete the plan despite injury or illness.

Every workout must have enough detail to be actionable.

Do not create unsupported exact claims about nutrition or training. Cite the research used to design the plan in a separate `TRAINING_SOURCES.md`.

* Ask whether workouts should be saved to HealthKit.

* Record the Endurance workout identity in metadata where supported.

1. Create a migration plan.
2. Add migration fixtures representing previous application versions.
3. Run migration tests.
4. Verify CloudKit compatibility when synchronization is enabled.
5. Verify extension compatibility.
6. Document irreversible changes.
7. Provide safe recovery behavior.

Never solve a migration problem by telling existing users to delete and reinstall the application.
